# Academic visual system

Use this reference for both the HTML review deck and final PPT. Adapt the palette and imagery to the research topic while preserving a formal academic context.

## Material and tone

Create a restrained, clean, paper-like surface rather than a blank white canvas. Use a low-saturation light base with subtle paper fiber, soft haze, embossing, or fine grain. The texture should be quiet enough that text, charts, equations, and evidence remain dominant.

Use one deep, stable structural color for titles, hierarchy, chart emphasis, and visual weight. Add one accent color sparingly for a key term, annotation, number, curve, or transition in the argument. Avoid large pink fields and other palettes that weaken the formality of an academic report. Deep tones carry structure, light tones create breathing room, and small warm or contrasting accents direct attention.

## Typography as structure

Use oversized titles, keywords, characters, numbers, or symbols as a background skeleton where appropriate. Reduce their visual pressure through grain, halftone diffusion, light blur, transparency, cropping, or edge dissolution. Foreground text must remain crisp and readable. Do not apply text shadows to body copy.

Foreground information may use precise editorial devices such as labels, compact notes, numbering, narrow tracking, short annotations, or a rare handwritten emphasis. Text should guide, decorate, and inform, but the deck must still read quickly in a presentation setting. Large titles are allowed; body copy stays concise.

## Depth and composition

Establish clear layers:

- background: quiet material texture and softened typographic forms;
- middle ground: restrained outlines, transparent ellipses, fine rules, or a subtle focus field;
- foreground: the decisive figure, chart, equation, image, or information group.

Use controlled asymmetry rather than a generic centered template. The visual center may sit rightward or lower, while an edge can carry a calm vertical information strip. Mild rotation, stacking, offset, or suspension can add physical depth, but academic evidence must remain aligned, comparable, and easy to read.

Use shadow only to express shallow paper depth or separation. Avoid heavy drop shadows, glows, bevels, and effects that render inconsistently. Blur belongs to background atmosphere, never to essential content.

## Information density

Balance compressed evidence with deliberate breathing room. Dense regions should feel like layered paper or organized data, not a dashboard of repeated cards. Sparse regions should support attention, not signal missing substance. Prefer one dominant visual and a small number of supporting annotations per slide.

Academic decks are summaries. Show enough method to judge validity and enough results to support the conclusion, but do not turn the deck into a laboratory manual. Conversely, do not imitate an empty strategy presentation with slogans and oversized whitespace. Every slide needs a clear informational job.

## Visual integrity

Use native charts, tables, equations, and required diagrams for editable evidence. Keep decorative assets subordinate. Distinguish measured results, simulations, conceptual diagrams, and planned work through labels and consistent visual treatment. Validate foreground readability against the textured background and inspect all slides after rendering.

## Motion and interactive tone

Motion belongs to the information hierarchy. Use it to reveal argument order, trace a signal or causal path, compare states, focus an image region, or show parameter sensitivity. Keep entry effects short and quiet; use fade, short slide, restrained zoom, and staggered reveal. Light sweeps, signal ripples, animated arrows, and curve interpolation must remain low-amplitude and subordinate to labels and evidence.

Do not use looping decoration, large rotations, bouncing interfaces, game-like particles, neon glows, or hover effects that are required to understand the result. Themes, charts, controls, highlights, and animation layers must all use the same semantic palette. Under reduced motion, replace movement with immediate state changes, persistent arrowheads, static paths, and visible final annotations.

# Speaker script rules

Use this reference only after the final PPT has been delivered and the user accepts the offer to create a speaker script.

## Language contract

Record the slide language and script language independently. Follow the user's explicit choice of Chinese, English, or bilingual output. If the user requests bilingual material, define whether the slides, the script, or both are bilingual; do not assume that two full languages will fit on every slide.

Build a small terminology map for technical terms, abbreviations, proper nouns, equations, and units. Use one approved translation consistently. Preserve the strength of claims across languages: hypothesis, association, causal conclusion, preliminary result, and completed result are not interchangeable.

## One-to-one slide mapping

Give each script section:

- final slide number and exact slide title;
- target speaking time;
- the point the audience must understand;
- natural spoken narration;
- a transition to the next slide when one is needed;
- pronunciation, emphasis, pause, or demonstration cues only when useful.
- explicit page-turn cues where the speaker should advance the slide.

Mark cover slides, section dividers, backup slides, videos, and slides intentionally passed without narration. Never let numbering drift silently.

## Spoken content

The script should add value that the slide cannot carry: explain the visual, expose the reasoning, define the comparison, qualify the claim, and connect the result to the research question. Do not read bullets, axes, tables, or citations line by line. Do not place unsupported facts only in the script; spoken claims require the same evidence tracking as visible claims.

Use a suitable hook, formal opening, natural transitions, and a concise conclusion. Avoid large numbers of long or syntactically nested sentences. Keep the language conversational enough to speak comfortably while remaining appropriate for a defense, academic report, or conference setting.

In the editable script, place delivery cues beside the relevant text: pauses, page turns, visual references, demonstrations, and emphasis. Highlight only the genuinely important spoken phrases; excessive highlighting destroys the hierarchy.

Use natural first-person wording only when it matches authorship and setting. Do not say "we" for work the presenter did not perform. Avoid ceremonial filler, inflated novelty claims, and scripted confidence that the evidence does not support.

## Timing

Use the presenter's measured speaking rate when available. Otherwise, use only a provisional drafting range of roughly 220–260 Chinese characters per minute or 120–140 English words per minute, then reserve time for pauses, figures, equations, demonstrations, and slide changes. These ranges do not prove the actual delivery time.

Allocate more time to the research gap, method choices, decisive evidence, and limitations than to the title or agenda. Keep a rehearsal buffer instead of filling the stated duration exactly. After drafting, total all slide times and shorten the argument before accelerating delivery.

## Output and synchronization

When supported, place each slide's script segment in its speaker notes. Also provide a separate editable script; prefer `.docx` when document authoring is available and `.md` otherwise.

After any material deck change, rerun a synchronization pass:

1. freeze the final slide order and titles;
2. update the script section numbers and transitions;
3. compare claims, values, terminology, and caveats against the evidence ledger;
4. recalculate the duration estimate;
5. ensure the delivered PPT and script belong to the same version.
